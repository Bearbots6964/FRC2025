Your template should correspond (roughly) with what your robot looks like and what you intend to do with it. While you can add features of another template to your project later, it is difficult and error-prone. Therefore, **make sure you have a clear idea of what your robot will look like**.
# AdvantageKit + Maple-Sim CTRE Swerve
- **Use with**: SDS MK4i swerve modules with Kraken motors, CANcoders, Pigeon 2.0, CANivore
- **Use for**: Competition codebases, codebases that require accurate simulation
- **Don't use for**: Simple projects
- **Get it**: [here](https://github.com/Shenzhen-Robotics-Alliance/AdvantageKit-TalonSwerveTemplate-MapleSim-Enhanced). Use `git clone` or download the code from GitHub.
This template uses AdvantageKit for logging and IO, and maple-sim for simulation. maple-sim uses a physics engine to give your robot realistic interactions with game elements and game pieces in a simulation.

After downloading the template, follow the instructions [here](https://docs.advantagekit.org/getting-started/template-projects/talonfx-swerve-template) to set everything up.
